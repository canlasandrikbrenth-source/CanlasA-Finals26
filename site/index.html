What was wrong, and what changed

An AI chat application is easy to start and easy to neglect. Out of the box, nothing would tell you it had stopped working, nothing would save your data, and nothing would stop a full disk from taking it down. This project treats that as the actual problem.

  • It now tells me when it is unhealthy. Nine alarms email me. When I deliberately filled the disk, the alarm arrived 5 minutes 30 seconds after the fault began, about two minutes before I noticed the app failing.
  • Its data can be recovered. The database is backed up to storage every hour. I deleted a real chat, restored from backup in 23 seconds, and the chat came back.
  • Its secrets are not in the code. Keys live in AWS Parameter Store, and the database cannot be reached from the internet.
  • Routine checks run without me. The backup and a 12-question test run on schedules. Across 15 scheduled test runs over two days, 14 scored 12 out of 12.
  • The system is honest about its limits. It refuses questions outside its documents and will not decide anything about a person.

What is not finished

I did not complete a run on my laptop, because Docker Desktop needed a Windows component I chose not to install. My account also appears to block console passwords for IAM users, so the separate IAM user cannot log in. Both are explained on the pages that cover them. The system runs on one server, and the timings below come from a single day of operation.
